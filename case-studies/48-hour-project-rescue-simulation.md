# 48-Hour Project Rescue Simulation

A fictional scenario showing how I would respond when a cross-functional programme starts to lose control.

> Synthetic portfolio exercise. No employer, customer or client information is used.

## Scenario

A technology-enabled operational transformation is six weeks behind plan.

- Three workstreams report different completion percentages.
- Two critical dependencies have no agreed owner.
- The steering committee receives long status decks but still cannot see what requires a decision.
- Engineering believes the problem is scope; Operations believes the problem is resourcing.
- A customer-facing milestone is four weeks away.
- The RAID log has not been meaningfully updated for three weeks.

The wrong response would be to schedule more status meetings.

## First 4 hours — establish the facts

I would ask for five things only:

1. committed external milestones;
2. workstream-level deliverables and acceptance criteria;
3. unresolved dependencies;
4. decisions waiting for sponsor input; and
5. work that is actually blocked today.

I would avoid debating percentage-complete figures until the underlying deliverables are clear.

### Immediate output

A one-page recovery view:

| Area | Current state | Immediate question |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Unclear | What must be delivered for the next external milestone? |
| Schedule | Low confidence | Which dates are evidence-based and which are assumptions? |
| Dependencies | Two unowned | Who can make each dependency move? |
| Decisions | Ageing | What needs sponsor intervention within 24 hours? |
| Resources | Disputed | Is capacity genuinely missing, or is work blocked elsewhere? |

## Hours 4–12 — separate causes from symptoms

I would run a focused recovery session with workstream leads, not a broad project meeting.

For each blocked deliverable:

**Deliverable → blocker → dependency/decision → owner → latest safe date → consequence if missed**

This usually exposes whether the programme has:

- a genuine capacity problem;
- uncontrolled scope;
- weak dependency management;
- slow decisions;
- unrealistic planning; or
- several of these at once.

## Hours 12–24 — produce a recovery plan

The recovery plan would contain only three categories:

### Protect
Items that must remain unchanged because they are externally committed or critical to business value.

### Change
Scope, sequencing, resourcing or governance changes required to make the protected outcome achievable.

### Escalate
Decisions that cannot be resolved by the delivery team and require sponsor intervention.

Example:

| Action | Owner | Deadline | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freeze non-critical feature requests | Product Lead | Today | Protect critical-path capacity |
| Assign API dependency owner | Engineering Director | Today | Remove unowned dependency |
| Re-sequence training after technical validation | PM | Tomorrow | Recover 4 delivery days |
| Sponsor decision on launch scope | Sponsor | Tomorrow 12:00 | Prevent further planning ambiguity |

## Hours 24–48 — reset governance

Once the recovery plan is agreed, I would change the operating rhythm temporarily:

- one short daily blocker/dependency review;
- written workstream updates before the meeting;
- decision log with decision owner and latest safe date;
- RAG based on evidence, not sentiment;
- executive update limited to variance, impact, decision and action.

## Example executive update

**Status: 🔴 At risk**

The customer milestone remains achievable only if two decisions are closed within 24 hours and non-critical scope is deferred. The primary issue is dependency and decision latency rather than confirmed resource shortage. Recovery actions are now owned and tracked against latest-safe dates.

**Decision required:** approve reduced launch scope by 12:00 tomorrow.  
**Impact if delayed:** customer milestone moves by approximately one week.  
**Next checkpoint:** 16:00 tomorrow after dependency validation.

## What this demonstrates

Project rescue is not about producing a more detailed plan. It is about rapidly restoring:

**facts → priorities → ownership → decisions → confidence.**

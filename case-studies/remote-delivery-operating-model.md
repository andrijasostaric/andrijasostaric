# Remote Delivery Operating Model

A practical operating model for project and programme delivery across distributed teams.

> This is a synthetic portfolio example based on general delivery principles and does not reproduce any employer's internal process.

## Design goals

A remote delivery model should reduce ambiguity, unnecessary meetings and hidden dependencies. It should make ownership, decisions and progress visible without turning the team into a reporting machine.

## The model

### 1. One source of truth

Use one agreed location for the integrated plan, RAID, decisions, key documents and status. The tool matters less than consistency.

### 2. Written-first coordination

Important updates should survive the meeting. Decisions, risks, changes and actions are documented so colleagues in different time zones can work asynchronously.

### 3. Explicit ownership

Every material deliverable, risk, dependency and decision has a named owner. Shared ownership is treated as a warning sign until accountability is clear.

### 4. Meetings only where synchronisation adds value

Use live time for:

- decisions;
- conflict resolution;
- complex cross-functional dependencies;
- workshops;
- stakeholder alignment.

Routine progress collection should be asynchronous where possible.

### 5. Escalation by exception

Leadership reporting focuses on variance, decisions and intervention needed — not a complete retelling of project activity.

### 6. Time-zone-aware delivery

Work should be structured so that teams can hand off cleanly across locations. Critical meetings should have written inputs beforehand and written outcomes afterwards.

## Example weekly rhythm

| Timing | Purpose | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Workstream updates and RAID refresh | Asynchronous |
| Tuesday | Dependency and blocker review | 30 min live |
| Wednesday | Deep work / workshops as needed | Exception-based |
| Thursday | Decision and sponsor escalation | 30 min live |
| Friday | Executive summary and next-week priorities | Written |

## What I would monitor

- milestone confidence;
- unresolved critical dependencies;
- ageing decisions;
- risk exposure;
- work blocked by unclear ownership;
- repeated operational issues;
- meeting load versus delivery value;
- actions carried over week to week.

## Principle

**Remote work succeeds when visibility comes from the operating system, not from people proving that they are online.**

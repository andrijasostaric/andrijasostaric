# 30–60–90 Day Delivery Plan

A concise example of how I approach a new project, programme or operational-transformation role.

> This is a general demonstration framework, not material from a current or former employer.

## First 30 days — understand and stabilise

**Objective:** create a reliable picture of the work before changing it.

- Map sponsors, decision-makers, delivery teams, customers and critical dependencies.
- Confirm scope, intended outcomes, success measures and non-negotiable constraints.
- Build or validate the integrated plan and milestone view.
- Establish a RAID log and identify risks that require immediate ownership.
- Clarify RACI and escalation paths.
- Review existing reporting, meetings and tooling; remove obvious duplication.
- Baseline delivery health: schedule, scope, dependencies, resources, decisions and operational readiness.

**Outputs:** stakeholder map, delivery baseline, RAID, RACI, milestone plan, governance cadence and initial executive summary.

## Days 31–60 — create control and momentum

**Objective:** turn the baseline into a working delivery system.

- Assign owners and due dates to open risks, decisions and dependencies.
- Introduce concise workstream reporting focused on variance and action.
- Resolve unclear ownership and recurring decision bottlenecks.
- Separate operational noise from genuine programme risks.
- Standardise documentation and create a single source of truth.
- Identify repetitive manual coordination that can be simplified or automated.
- Agree meaningful measures for progress, quality and readiness.

**Outputs:** stable governance, cleaner reporting, prioritised remediation plan, decision log, dependency management and improvement backlog.

## Days 61–90 — improve and scale

**Objective:** make delivery more predictable and less dependent on manual follow-up.

- Analyse recurring blockers and redesign the underlying process where practical.
- Improve dashboards and executive reporting around outcomes rather than activity.
- Automate low-value coordination or evidence collection where appropriate.
- Build reusable templates and knowledge assets.
- Test operational readiness and handover requirements.
- Establish lessons learned and measurable improvement targets.

**Outputs:** improved operating model, reusable delivery toolkit, automation opportunities, readiness plan and clear next-quarter priorities.

## How I measure whether the approach is working

I look for evidence that:

1. people know what they own;
2. critical dependencies are visible before they become blockers;
3. decisions are made at the right level and recorded;
4. reporting helps leaders act rather than simply informing them;
5. delivery becomes more predictable over time; and
6. the process requires less manual chasing, not more.

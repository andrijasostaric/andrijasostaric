# Executive Decision Memo — Example

A fictional one-page decision format for senior stakeholders.

> Synthetic portfolio example. The purpose is to demonstrate concise programme communication.

## Decision required

Approve a phased rollout rather than a single full-scope launch.

## Recommendation

Proceed with **Phase 1** on the existing target date using the validated core scope. Move two non-critical capabilities into **Phase 2**, four weeks later.

## Why this decision is needed now

The programme has one unresolved technical dependency and one operational-readiness dependency. Keeping both non-critical capabilities in the first release would consume the remaining schedule contingency and increase the likelihood of delaying the externally committed milestone.

## Options

| Option | Benefit | Risk | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-scope launch | All capabilities available immediately | High schedule risk; no remaining contingency | No |
| Phased launch | Protects committed milestone; preserves quality and readiness | Requires second release and additional coordination | **Yes** |
| Delay full launch | Maximum preparation time | Misses committed milestone and delays business value | No |

## Impact of recommended option

**Schedule:** core launch date protected.  
**Scope:** two non-critical capabilities deferred by four weeks.  
**Cost:** limited additional coordination; no major new spend assumed.  
**Customer impact:** core service remains available as committed.  
**Operational risk:** lower than full-scope launch because readiness can be validated in stages.

## Decision deadline

**12:00 tomorrow.**

A later decision would reduce the benefit of phased delivery because teams would continue preparing both launch paths in parallel.

## Actions after approval

1. Freeze Phase 1 scope.
2. Update integrated plan and dependencies.
3. Confirm Phase 2 owners and target date.
4. Communicate the revised release structure to affected stakeholders.
5. Track Phase 1 readiness daily until launch.

---

### Communication principle

Senior stakeholders usually do not need more project detail. They need the **decision, options, consequence and latest safe date** in a form they can act on quickly.
